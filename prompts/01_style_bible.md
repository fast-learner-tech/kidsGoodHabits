# Style Bible: kidsGoodHabitsCenter

Paste the **STYLE BLOCK** at the start of every image prompt so every character, set and prop looks like the same show.

## Look
- Stylized 3D animated feature-film look: soft rounded shapes, big expressive eyes, simple readable silhouettes.
- Soft global lighting, warm key light, gentle rim light, no harsh shadows, no dark or scary scenes.
- Saturated but friendly palette. Each episode gets one dominant color (see color script in the guide).
- Slightly exaggerated proportions: head about 1/3 of body height for the youngest characters.
- Original designs only. Never reference or imitate a specific studio's characters. Do not put studio or brand names in prompts.

## Inclusivity rules (worldwide audience)
1. Cast shows a range of skin tones, hair textures and body shapes. No stereotypes.
2. Clothing is everyday and neutral (t-shirts, dungarees, pyjamas). No religious symbols, national flags, or region-specific costume as the default.
3. Food, homes and chores are shown in a general way (a bowl of porridge, a bed, a sink) so any family recognises them. Use background details sparingly and varied.
4. No on-screen text in the story world, so the film works in any language. Meaning is carried by visuals, music and voice-over.
5. No gender-based chores or roles. Any parent figure can cook, tidy, or fix things.
6. Family structures are not specified: "grown-up" characters can be read as parent, grandparent or carer.
7. Positive tone only: no shaming, no yelling, no punishment. Habits are shown as fun and rewarding.

## Camera and motion rules for little viewers
- Shots last 3-8 seconds, slow gentle camera moves, no flashing lights or strobing (also an accessibility safety rule).
- One idea per scene. Repeat key actions three times (kids learn by repetition).
- Characters look at the camera and talk to the viewer at key moments ("Can you do it too?").
- Calm music, clear voice, no sudden loud sounds.
